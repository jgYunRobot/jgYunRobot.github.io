# Project Nodus

## Direction

A robotics atelier with an invariant dark home palette: a lit, extruded metal wordmark above a floating filmstrip of actual projects. The home is an exhibition entrance; article pages remain readable technical documents. Inspired by the restraint, real photography and fine dividers in the BMW M design analysis, without copying its identity or typography.

- Dark palette: ink #080b0e, cool white #edf1f3, muted #a3aeb7, mint #9ed8c5.
- Home always uses the authored dark palette, silver title and mint N regardless of stored theme or OS/browser preference. The initial HTML, pre-paint bootstrap, navigation handler, CSS tokens, poster and WebGL materials agree. Home declares a dark color scheme and matching browser theme-color from the first response. The theme picker is offered on reading pages, where System/Light/Dark and saved preferences still apply; visiting home never overwrites that preference.
- Solid home/card surfaces retain their semantic HTML and CSS fallback but are painted once into stretched 1x1 sRGB 2D canvases. An on-device Android 16 / Samsung Internet 30.0.5.27 comparison found force-dark changed CSS ink #080b0e to #000306 and an SVG image to #25282b, while the 2D canvas retained #080b0e like Chrome. These canvases are decorative, ignore pointer input and do not run animation loops. Loaded cover images also get one sRGB thumbnail paint at a maximum 768px width, then swap visibility without a loading fade. The original image and alt text remain in the DOM; video previews keep their native video elements. This avoids Samsung image dimming without a per-frame video-copy loop.
- Card and ground shadows use small baked black/alpha WebP textures, shared by all six cards, instead of CSS box-shadow or runtime blur. Multiply blending keeps these shadow layers and media vignettes from adding a light halo during compositing. Card and badge borders use opaque dark colours. A fixed home palette removes the light-to-forced-dark mismatch; The connected Android 16 / Samsung Internet 30.0.5.27 handset retained ink #080b0e and card #161d23 in both bright and dark page-view modes, with the previous white halo removed. Other browser versions and forced filters still require separate acceptance; this is not a universal page-CSS opt-out. Regenerate shadow textures with node scripts/generate-home-shadows.mjs.
- The page context menu is suppressed through one document listener, retained across Astro navigation. Cross-origin player menus are owned by their embedded provider.
- Title: real Three.js beveled text, fixed silver front, white key and rim lights, ground shadows. Two lines on all screen sizes, with only the N highlighted in mint; gentle pointer response.
- Title startup: show an inline transparent WebP poster from the real Three.js scene in the initial HTML, including separate desktop/mobile dark frames. The poster needs no JavaScript, web font or additional image request. Keep it visible until the first fully laid-out WebGL frame, then switch both layers atomically without a crossfade. Match the poster and live camera with the same contain fit and resting pose; start idle motion at zero after reveal. JavaScript-disabled or failed renderers retain the poster, and navigation cancels pending reveal work.
- Home language: English copy, navigation and accessibility labels. English card metadata lives alongside the original article metadata; article text and language remain intact.
- Timeline: the centered card’s date appears below the deck as YYYY.MM.DD in the configured site timezone. Existing posts have source-compared approximate last-work dates in homeDate; new posts default to the blog registration date (pubDatetime). The deck sorts oldest to newest from left to right by this date, independent of later edits. It opens on the latest project and wraps at both ends as a circular carousel. Digit reels roll on date changes, settle on the exact date, and respect pause/reduced-motion preferences.
- Gallery: project video stills, descriptive titles, categories and dates. Perspective makes the current card central and side cards recede. Each card floats independently and lifts on hover/focus.
- Cards link directly to posts. Hover plays only the pointed card's muted preview; leaving, switching cards, hiding the tab or moving offscreen stops it. Local previews are short, silent, compressed excerpts. All current projects, including Torque Control, use local previews. The Torque Control article serves the user-provided full original MP4 unchanged.
- Browse with previous/next buttons, arrow keys, mouse wheel over the cards, drag/swipe and position dots. Mobile keeps one main card plus side previews; touch has an explicit preview toggle and immediate article links.
- Native anchors, meaningful image alt text, semantic navigation, focus outlines and a live position label. Motion can be paused; reduced-motion disables idle movement and automatic hover previews.
- No autoplay carousel. WebGL and JavaScript absence keep the title poster and project links visible. WebGL runs only on home, caps pixel ratio and pauses when out of view or hidden. Astro navigation disposes GPU resources and listeners.
- Performance: transform/opacity-only card and digit motion, one animation-frame write per drag frame, paused offscreen/background/distant cards, and no repeat gallery layout on unchanged size. Text geometry uses 8 curve segments and 2 bevel segments (13,432 triangles, down from 22,144); pixel ratio caps are 1.5 on desktop and 1.25 on mobile. The title targets 60 Hz on desktop and 30 Hz on mobile with time-based smoothing.
- New posts carry cover/preview fields in frontmatter. Existing article text, sources and routes stay intact.

## Verification

Check stored System/Light/Dark preferences and simulated light/dark system signals all retain the same dark home before and after loading; verify reading-page themes and navigation still work. Check the lit title, individual lift, video exclusivity, carousel buttons/keyboard/swipe, direct article navigation, return-to-home lifecycle, mobile overflow and pause/reduced-motion behavior. Build and local-link checks are separate from visual and interaction checks.
